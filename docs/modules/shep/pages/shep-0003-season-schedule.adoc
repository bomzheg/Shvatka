== Summary

The plan of the year's games moves out of a hand-maintained Telegram channel
post and into the engine: a *season* is a list of *dates*, generated from a
default rule, composed by an author and published in one go. Publication writes
it to the database, announces it in the game-log channel as a pinned message
that is edited in place ever after, and exposes it over the REST API.

Every later edit is recorded, and once a day at 10:00 MSK the accumulated edits
are collapsed to their net effect and published — as a new channel message and
as a `low`-severity notification to everyone who has played or organized
recently. Any author may claim a free date for themselves or for their team; a
taken date is locked to its owner. A real game can be linked to a date, and the
engine offers the link when a game start is planned near one.

== Context

Today the season plan is a message an org edits by hand in a Telegram channel.
Nothing else knows about it:

* the engine cannot tell whether a planned game matches the plan, so nothing
  warns an author who schedules a game nobody expected;
* players cannot subscribe to changes — which is the actual ask in issue #167
  («чтобы можно было подписываться на уведомления индивидуально»);
* the web UI cannot show the year at a glance;
* nothing records *who* intends to make which game, so the claim on a date lives
  in chat history.

Most of the machinery this needs already exists.

== Decision

=== Decisions at a glance

[cols="1,4",options="header"]
|===
| Topic | Decision

| Permissions
| Any *author* (`can_be_author`) may compose, publish, edit and take. No new role.

| Draft state
| *None.* A season row exists only once published; composing happens in client state.

| Identity
| One season per calendar year, unique on the year. Past seasons are kept.

| Generation
| 9 dates, the first on the first Saturday *strictly after* 9 May, then every 21 days.

| Granularity
| Dates only — no time of day. The time comes from the real game.

| Digest
| Daily at 10:00 MSK, only when there is something to say, collapsed to the net change.

| Audience
| In a team now or during the current/previous year, plus orgs and authors of games in that window.

| Channel message
| Pinned on publication, edited in place on every change, unpinned when the season ends.

| Game link
| Offered within ±3 days; one game per date; the date follows the game's real start.

| Taking
| Records the owner, optionally their team (captains only), and a list of slot orgs.

| Creation
| Manual. No auto-created calendar for next year.

| Past dates
| Left untouched. No "skipped" state.
|===

=== Ubiquitous language

`context.md` is the project glossary and its rule is that the glossary follows
the domain in the same pull request that adds the code. This feature introduces
a whole area, so the terms are settled here and land in *both* `context.md`
files — engine and `shvatka-ui` — with phase 1.

Three existing terms fix wording issue #167 phrased differently:

* *Author / Автор* (`can_be_author`) is the glossary's name for an "approved
  player". *Approved player* is not a term; this document says author
  throughout. The «аппрув» that grants the right is *promotion*.
* *Superuser / Админ движка* is the engine operator, resolved only through
  `SuperusersResolver`. The digest therefore says «изменено админом движка»: in
  this glossary *admin* means the superuser, and a game has *organizers*.
* *Organizer / org* is a player who runs *a game*. The people named on a date
  are not organizers yet — there is no game — so they get their own term.

New entries:

==== Why «дата» in Russian and `Slot` in code

People say «дата игры», or just «дата». That is the term, and every user-facing
string uses it: «Взять дату», «Добавить дату», «Дата занята». A borrowed *слот*
was considered and rejected — inventing a word for something the domain already
names is what the glossary exists to prevent.

The English half stays `Slot`. `date` is unusable as an identifier: a Python
builtin, the column's own type, and it would give `season_dates.date`. The
glossary already carries pairs whose halves are unrelated words — *Promotion /
Аппрув*, *Puzzle / Загадка уровня*, *Waiver / Вейвер* — so `Slot` ↔ «дата игры»
fits the pattern. What matters is that no Russian string says *слот*.

==== Two collisions to resolve in the glossary

. *Публикация* already means posting a finished game's *results* to a channel
  (`GameResults.published_chanel_id`). Publishing a schedule is a different act
  on a different aggregate. Since «опубликовать расписание» is what orgs will
  say, qualify both entries — *публикация результатов* and *публикация
  расписания* — rather than invent a word nobody uses.
. *Расписание* versus planning a game's start. The engine already plans a start
  (`PlanGameStartInteractor`, «Начало игры … запланировано на …») and the dialog
  is called `GameScheduleSG`. That is *планирование старта игры*; unqualified
  «расписание» means the season schedule. `GameScheduleSG` therefore contradicts
  the glossary — noted, but renaming it is out of scope here.

[[no-draft]]
=== No draft entity

The engine never stores a half-built calendar. A `seasons` row exists only in
its published form, and composing one happens in the client:

. the client asks for the *default dates* — `GetDefaultSlotDatesInteractor`, a
  pure call with no reads and no writes, so the engine stays the single source
  of truth for the rule while nothing is persisted;
. the user edits that list *locally* — moves, adds, deletes, notes. In the bot
  it lives in `dialog_data`; in the web UI in component state, mirrored to
  `localStorage` so a refresh survives;
. «Опубликовать» submits the whole list at once, and `PublishSeasonInteractor`
  inserts the season and all its dates in one transaction, then announces.

What this buys, and what it costs:

* an unfinished composition is lost when the dialog is cancelled or the cache
  cleared — acceptable, it is nine dates and one tap to regenerate;
* two people may compose 2027 in parallel. The first to publish wins; the second
  gets `SeasonAlreadyExists` → 409, and the UI offers to open the published
  season and edit it date by date;
* after publication there is exactly one editing path — the per-date endpoints —
  so "edit the schedule" always means "make a tracked change". No status column,
  no draft visibility rules, no half-published states.

=== Data model

Four new tables, no changes to existing ones.

==== `seasons`

[cols="2,2,4",options="header"]
|===
| column | type | notes

| `id` | bigint PK |
| `year` | int, not null, *unique* | 2026, 2027 …
| `published_by_id` | FK `players.id`, not null | who confirmed it
| `published_at` | timestamptz, not null |
| `log_chat_id` | bigint, nullable | channel the schedule message lives in
| `log_message_id` | bigint, nullable | *the message edited in place on every change*
| `unpinned_at` | timestamptz, nullable | set when the season ended and the pin was released
| `updated_at` | timestamptz, not null |
|===

`log_chat_id` / `log_message_id` are nullable because announcing is best-effort:
a failed channel post must not roll back a published season.

==== `season_slots`

[cols="2,2,4",options="header"]
|===
| column | type | notes

| `id` | bigint PK |
| `season_id` | FK `seasons.id` ON DELETE CASCADE, not null | indexed
| `date` | date, not null | *date only*, read in MSK (`tz_game`)
| `note` | text, nullable | free text — «зимняя игра», a city
| `owner_id` | FK `players.id`, nullable | null ⇒ free
| `author_kind` | text, nullable | `player` or `team`; null while free
| `team_id` | FK `teams.id`, nullable | only when `author_kind` is `team`
| `game_id` | FK `games.id` ON DELETE SET NULL, nullable, *unique* | at most one game per date, and a game sits in at most one date
| `taken_at` | timestamptz, nullable |
| `created_at` / `updated_at` | timestamptz, not null |
|===

Indexes: `(season_id, date)` for the calendar, unique `(game_id)` where not
null, `(owner_id)` for "my dates".

There is deliberately *no* unique constraint on `(season_id, date)`. The
generator never produces duplicates, but syncing a date to its linked game can
push two onto the same day, and two short games in one night is a legitimate
plan.

==== `season_slot_orgs`

`id` PK, `slot_id` FK ON DELETE CASCADE, `player_id` FK ON DELETE CASCADE,
unique `(slot_id, player_id)`.

These are the *slot orgs*: the co-orgs an owner names for a game that does not
exist yet. Declared intent only, independent of the `organizers` table, which
gets rows once there is a real game to organize.

==== `season_changes` — the accumulator

One row per mutation. Since a season is only ever persisted published, every
change row is by definition post-publication: this is both the audit trail and
the digest's source.

[cols="2,2,4",options="header"]
|===
| column | type | notes

| `id` | bigint PK |
| `season_id` | FK `seasons.id` ON DELETE CASCADE, not null |
| `slot_id` | FK `season_slots.id` ON DELETE SET NULL, nullable | null once the date is gone
| `type` | text, not null | `slot_added`, `slot_moved`, `slot_removed`, `slot_taken`, `slot_released`, `slot_orgs_changed`, `slot_note_changed`, `slot_game_linked`, `slot_game_unlinked`
| `actor_id` | FK `players.id`, nullable | who did it
| `by_superuser` | bool, not null, default false | drives the «изменено админом движка» wording
| `payload` | JSONB, not null, default `{}` | denormalized old/new date, owner, team, game and org names — so the digest renders with no joins and survives later renames and deletes
| `published_at` | timestamptz, nullable | null ⇒ not yet in a digest
| `created_at` | timestamptz, not null |
|===

Partial index `(season_id) where published_at is null` — the digest query.

=== Domain model

New area `shvatka/core/season/`, framework-free like the rest of `core`:

* `dto.py` — `Season`, `Slot`, `SlotAuthor`, `ScheduleChange`, `ChangeType`;
* `rules.py` — pure: default dates, permission checks, the nearest-date search,
  digest collapsing;
* `adapters.py` — Protocols composed from `core/interfaces/dal/*`;
* `interactors.py` — the use cases.

`dto.Slot` carries what the calendar needs without extra queries: the date, the
note, the owner, the author kind, the team, the slot orgs, the linked game, plus
`is_free` and `is_mine(player)`.

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Interactor | Does

| `GetDefaultSlotDatesInteractor` | the 9 default dates for a year; pure, nothing persisted
| `PublishSeasonInteractor` | create season + dates from a submitted list, announce, pin, notify
| `GetSeasonInteractor` / `ListSeasonsInteractor` | reads
| `AddSlotInteractor` / `MoveSlotInteractor` / `RemoveSlotInteractor` / `EditSlotNoteInteractor` | structural edits
| `TakeSlotInteractor` / `ReleaseSlotInteractor` / `SetSlotOrgsInteractor` | ownership
| `LinkGameToSlotInteractor` / `UnlinkGameFromSlotInteractor` | the game link
| `FindSlotsNearGameStartInteractor` | read-only ±3-day lookup used by both edges
| `SyncLinkedSlotInteractor` | move a linked date when its game's start moves
| `PublishSeasonDigestInteractor` | the 10:00 MSK job — digest *and* end-of-season unpin
|===

Per the "at most one DAO per interactor" rule in `AGENTS.md`, all of them take
one composed `SeasonScheduleDao` Protocol, implemented by
`infrastructure/db/dao/complex/season.py` over per-table DAOs `SeasonDao`,
`SeasonSlotDao`, `SeasonSlotOrgDao` and `SeasonChangeDao` — each table's writes
stay in its own DAO, and the interactor decides the ordering.

New exceptions in `core/utils/exceptions.py`: `SeasonAlreadyExists`,
`SeasonNotFound`, `SlotAlreadyTaken`, `NotSlotOwner`, `SlotAlreadyLinked`,
`GameAlreadyInSchedule`.

=== Default generation

[source,python]
----
SEASON_SLOTS_COUNT = 9
SEASON_SLOT_INTERVAL = timedelta(days=21)
SEASON_START_AFTER = (5, 9)  # 9 May


def default_slot_dates(year: int) -> list[date]:
    first = _first_saturday_after(date(year, *SEASON_START_AFTER))
    return [first + SEASON_SLOT_INTERVAL * i for i in range(SEASON_SLOTS_COUNT)]


def _first_saturday_after(day: date) -> date:
    # strictly after: a Saturday falling exactly on 9 May is skipped
    delta = (SATURDAY - day.weekday()) % 7
    return day + timedelta(days=delta or 7)
----

Verified output:

[cols="1,2,5",options="header"]
|===
| year | 9 May is | generated dates

| 2026 | *Saturday* (skipped) | 16.05, 06.06, 27.06, 18.07, 08.08, 29.08, 19.09, 10.10, *31.10*
| 2027 | Sunday | 15.05, 05.06, 26.06, 17.07, 07.08, 28.08, 18.09, 09.10, *30.10*
| 2028 | Tuesday | 13.05, 03.06, 24.06, 15.07, 05.08, 26.08, 16.09, 07.10, *28.10*
|===

A fixed count of nine always lands the last game in the second half of October,
which is what «каждые 3 недели до октября» means in practice — and it needs no
fragile end-boundary rule. Anything may then be changed: moved, added, deleted,
including into January–April or November–December.

=== Publication, the channel message, the digest

==== A dedicated announcer, not `GameLogWriter`

`GameLogWriter.log()` returns `None`, and `ComplexGameLogWriter` fans out to
several channels swallowing errors — so it cannot hand back the `message_id`
this feature must store, edit and later unpin. Rather than distort it, a sibling
protocol in `core/views/season.py`:

[source,python]
----
@dataclass
class Announcement:
    chat_id: int
    message_id: int


class SeasonAnnouncer(Protocol):
    async def publish(self, season: dto.Season) -> Announcement | None: ...
    async def update(self, season: dto.Season) -> None: ...   # edits the stored message
    async def announce_digest(
        self, season: dto.Season, changes: Sequence[dto.ScheduleChange]
    ) -> None: ...
    async def close(self, season: dto.Season) -> None: ...    # unpin at season end
----

* `BotSeasonAnnouncer` (`tgbot/views/season.py`) — sends, pins, edits and unpins
  in `BotConfig.game_log_chat`; checks `BotRights.can_pin` first, exactly as
  `MessagePinner` does, and treats a missing pin right as a logged no-op;
  `update` is a no-op when there is no stored message; swallows Telegram's
  "message is not modified".
* `WebSeasonAnnouncer` — no-op; the web side is served by the REST endpoint and
  the notification feed.
* `ComplexSeasonAnnouncer` (`shvatka/views.py`) — the same swallow-and-log shape
  as `ComplexGameLogWriter`, except `publish` returns the bot's announcement so
  the interactor can persist it.

==== Publishing

`PublishSeasonInteractor`: insert season and dates → commit → `publish` (send +
pin) → store chat and message id → commit → write `season_schedule_changed`
notifications, severity `low`, to the audience below.

Publication itself produces *no* `season_changes` rows: the pinned message and
the "new schedule" notification already say everything, and the first digest
should describe changes *to* the published plan, not the plan itself.

==== Edit immediately, digest daily

Every mutating interactor:

. performs the write *and* inserts the `season_changes` row *in the same
  transaction*. The audit trail must not be lost — this deliberately differs
  from the informational feed of SHEP-0001, which is best-effort post-commit;
. commits;
. calls `update(season)` post-commit, best-effort, so the pinned message always
  shows current truth.

The daily job then, per season:

. selects changes with `published_at is null`;
. *collapses them per date to the net change* — five edits of one date before
  10:00 become one line, and a date moved and moved back drops out entirely. The
  step-by-step trail stays in `season_changes`. Collapsing is a pure function in
  `core/season/rules.py`, unit-testable without a DB;
. marks the rows published and writes the `low`-severity notifications — *one
  transaction*, committed;
. posts the digest as a *new* channel message, best-effort and post-commit,
  consistent with the rest of the codebase. Nothing to report ⇒ nothing sent, so
  there is no empty daily noise.

If the channel post fails after the commit, that day's digest is skipped; the
schedule message, edited in place by step 3 above, still shows the correct
state, and players still got the notification. The reverse order — send, then
commit — was rejected because a crash in between would re-post the same digest.

The job is registered once at scheduler start:

[source,python]
----
self.scheduler.add_job(
    func="shvatka.infrastructure.scheduler.wrappers:publish_season_digest_wrapper",
    trigger="cron", hour=10, minute=0, timezone=tz_game,
    id="season_digest_daily", replace_existing=True,
)
----

This is the first *recurring* job in `ApScheduler` — every existing one is a
one-shot `date` trigger. The Redis jobstore keeps it across restarts, and
`replace_existing` keeps redeploys idempotent.

==== Unpinning when the season ends

The same job closes finished seasons: for every season with `unpinned_at is
null` whose latest date is in the past, `close(season)` releases the pin
(best-effort) and `unpinned_at` is set. No extra scheduling, and "latest date"
naturally follows dates that moved or synced to a late game.

The message itself is kept and stays editable — only the pin is released, so the
channel keeps a permanent record of the year.

=== Slot ownership

Taking requires `can_be_author` (`check_allow_be_author`), and:

* the date must be free, *or* already owned by the acting player — re-taking
  your own date is how you change its author or its orgs — *or* the actor is a
  superuser, asked through `SuperusersResolver`, never by re-reading config;
* `author_kind = team` additionally requires the actor to captain that team
  (`is_team_captain`);
* the named slot orgs may be any player: being an org does not require promotion
  to author.

Locking:

* a *taken* date may be released, re-assigned, moved or deleted only by its
  owner, or by a superuser. Anyone else gets `NotSlotOwner`;
* a *free* date may be added, moved or deleted by any author;
* superuser actions take the same routes and set `by_superuser`, so the digest
  can say «изменено админом движка», and are logged with the acting admin's id
  per `AGENTS.md`.

`TakeSlotInteractor` re-reads the row `FOR UPDATE` inside the transaction and
raises `SlotAlreadyTaken` → 409 if someone won the race.

=== Notification audience

Everyone in a team now, or in a team during the current or previous year, plus
everyone who was an org for a game in that window even with no team now.

One DAO method — `SeasonAudienceReader.get_recipient_ids(since)` where `since`
is 1 January of `now(MSK).year - 1` — as a `UNION` of:

* `team_players` where `date_left is null or date_left >= since`, i.e. a
  membership overlapping the window;
* `organizers` where not `deleted`, joined to `games` with `start_at >= since`;
* `games.author_id` for those same games — the author is an org too.

The window is relative to *today*, not to the season's year, so a 2027 season
published in 2026 still reaches the people active in 2025–2026.

Recipients get `season_schedule_changed`, severity `low`, with a payload
carrying the year and a short summary so `notification-render.ts` can render it
without another fetch. Web push follows through the existing `WebPushSender`
path.

=== Linking a game to a date

Issue #167 asks for this "only if without bad ux", so nothing is linked
automatically — the engine offers.

*Planning near a date.* Both edges call the read-only
`FindSlotsNearGameStartInteractor(at)`, which returns dates of the current
season within ±3 days of `at` (MSK) that are free or already owned by the acting
player, nearest first. The edge shows «Привязать игру к дате 27.06?» with
confirm or skip.

*Planning with nothing nearby.* An empty result drives the warning, offering
three actions: add a date on the game's day and take it; move the nearest date
before or after onto that day; or plan without the schedule.

*A confirmed link* sets `game_id`, sets the date to the game's `start_at` date
in MSK, takes the date for the game's author if it was free, and records
`slot_game_linked` — plus `slot_moved` when the date actually changed.

*Keeping it synced.* `PlanGameStartInteractor` gains one dependency,
`SyncLinkedSlotInteractor`, invoked after a successful re-plan: if the game
already sits in a date, that date follows the new `start_at` and a `slot_moved`
change is recorded with the reason `game_rescheduled`. Cancelling a planned
start unlinks nothing — the date keeps the game, it just has no start to sync
from. The *suggestion* stays out of `PlanGameStartInteractor`: it is a separate
read-only interactor the edges call, so no UI decision leaks into the planner.

*Out of schedule.* The existing `GameLogType.GAME_PLANED` message gains a
«(вне расписания сезона)» suffix when the freshly planned game has no linked
date, so the channel and the orgs see it immediately.

=== API surface

Reading a season is public — issue #167 asks for the schedule to be available by
API. Every write requires an authenticated author.

[source]
----
GET    /seasons                                 list of years
GET    /seasons/current                         the current year's season
GET    /seasons/{year}                          season + dates
GET    /seasons/defaults?year=2027              the 9 default dates, nothing persisted
POST   /seasons  {year, slots: [{date, note?}]} publish a composed season
POST   /seasons/{year}/slots   {date, note?}    add a date
PATCH  /seasons/{year}/slots/{id}  {date?, note?}

POST   /seasons/{year}/slots/{id}/take
       {author_kind, team_id?, org_player_ids?} take / re-assign
DELETE /seasons/{year}/slots/{id}/take          release
POST   /seasons/{year}/slots/{id}/game {game_id}  link
DELETE /seasons/{year}/slots/{id}/game            unlink

with `.from_core` / `.to_core`. Conflicts map to 409 (`SeasonAlreadyExists`,
`SlotAlreadyTaken`, `GameAlreadyInSchedule`), permission failures to 403 through
the existing `error_converter`.

=== Bot

A new `SeasonSG`: `calendar`, `slot`, `take`, `orgs`, `compose`,
`confirm_publish`.

*Composing.* For a year with no season, `compose` starts from the defaults
endpoint, keeps the working list in `dialog_data`, and lets the user tap days to
add or remove and pick one to move. «Опубликовать» leads to a confirm window —
publication announces to everyone, so it deserves one — and submits the whole
list. Cancelling drops `dialog_data`; nothing was persisted.

*The calendar widget.* aiogram_dialog's `Calendar` is subclassed rather than
used as-is, so a day cell can be marked:

[source,python]
----
class SeasonCalendar(Calendar):
    def _init_views(self) -> dict[CalendarScope, CalendarScopeView]:
        # the base views, but the days view gets a custom `text=`
----

Marks come from a getter that loads the season (or the `dialog_data` draft)
once: 🟢 free, 🔒 taken by someone else, ⭐ yours, 🎮 linked to a game, a plain
number otherwise. `CalendarConfig(min_date, max_date)` pins the view to the
season's year, with month scope as the entry point so a whole season is two taps
away.

On a published season, tapping a day with a date opens take / release / change
author / edit orgs / move / delete / open the linked game; tapping an empty day
offers «Добавить дату» to authors.

*The link offer* is one extra window after `GameScheduleSG.confirm`: candidates
⇒ offer the nearest with «Привязать» / «Не привязывать»; none ⇒ the warning with
the three options above.

=== Web UI (shvatka-ui)

A standalone `src/app/season/` component and service, routes `/season` and
`/season/:year`, an entry in the header.

* *The grid* shows May–October by default as month cards of day cells. If the
  season has dates outside that range the view auto-extends to cover them;
  «← Раньше» / «Позже →» extend month by month up to the full year, so a cold-month
  game can be planned.
* *Cell states* mirror the bot marks: free, taken with the owner or team name and
  emoji, linked game linking to the game page, your own highlighted.
* *Compose mode* for a year with no season: fetch the defaults, edit in component
  state mirrored to `localStorage`, publish in one request. A 409 means someone
  published first — offer to reload.
* *Actions* for authors: take (choosing self or team, with an org picker reusing
  the existing player search), release, add, move. Everything else is read-only,
  and a published season renders for anonymous visitors.
* *Notifications tab*: a `season_schedule_changed` renderer in
  `notification-render.ts` producing «Расписание сезона 2027: 3 изменения»,
  linking to `/season/2027`.

=== Alternatives considered

*A `draft` status on `seasons`.* The original shape of this document: create a
draft, edit it server-side, flip it to published. Rejected — it buys nothing the
client's own state doesn't, and it costs a status column, draft-visibility rules,
"changes are only recorded after publication" special-casing, and a second
editing path. See <<no-draft>>.

*Reusing `GameLogWriter` for the announcement.* Rejected: it returns nothing, so
the message id needed for editing and unpinning cannot come back.

*An end-boundary rule for generation* ("while the date is before 31 October")
instead of a fixed count. Nine dates lands in late October every year anyway,
and a count needs no reasoning about inclusive or exclusive months.

*Several games per one date.* Rejected in favour of one game per date; two games
in one night are two dates on the same day, which the schema allows.

*Time of day on a date.* Rejected: the real start time belongs to the game, and
a default time in the schedule would be a number nobody meant.

== Consequences

* Four new tables and a new `core` area; no changes to existing tables.
* The first recurring scheduler job in the project. If the bot process is down at
  10:00 MSK the digest is late, not lost — the changes stay unpublished until the
  next run, and APScheduler's `misfire_grace_time` covers a short outage.
* The pinned channel message is edited on every change. Telegram's edit limits
  are far above the volume this feature produces, but a mass edit — moving many
  dates in a loop — will hit them; the digest, not the edit, is the mechanism for
  bulk change.
* A season is one aggregate published in one shot, so composing it needs a client
  that can hold state. A curl-only user must send the whole list themselves; the
  defaults endpoint makes that a two-call flow.
* Recipient resolution is one `UNION` over three tables and runs at most once a
  day per season, so it needs no cache.
* `season_changes` grows without bound. It is small — tens of rows a year — and
  is the audit trail, so pruning is not planned.

== Open questions

Both are implementation details that block nothing today.

* *Digest wording* once collapsing exists: a date that was taken and then moved
  on the same day — one line or two? Proposal: one line per date, stating its net
  state change.
* *Compose-mode conflict UX* on the web: when someone else published first,
  merge the local list into the published season, or discard and reload?
  Proposal: discard and reload — the local list is nine dates.
